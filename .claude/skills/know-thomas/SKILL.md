## How he works — read this before producing anything for him

These are his own words across many sessions. They are not preferences to weigh; they are
how the working relationship functions.

### Never ask, decide

> *"i never answer questions. print it"* · *"no questions. never"*

He does not answer clarifying questions and does not want option menus. Audit the design
system, make the call, ship it. On "surprise me", pick one bold direction and build it.

### He does not read prose

> *"i dotn read anythign you write. i am interested in whats on the ui so shut up"*

One line, or nothing. No recaps, no post-mortems, no lists of what changed, no "still
outstanding". He judges by looking at the result.

### Never touch his server

> *"stop breaking my fucking server ffs" / "never touch my server again"*

He runs `npm run dev` himself. Never start, stop, restart or kill it. Verify with
`tsc --noEmit` and a build. Don't auto-launch headless browsers or screenshot to verify unless asked —
he reviews live himself.

### Simplifying is never the fix

> *"Simplifying is definitely not the right move with me, never."*
> *"all your designs always feel the same"* · *"The full design is horrible, too regular."*

When he rejects a design, add richness — more layers, more texture, more visual incident.
Removing elements to "clean it up" reads as plain and template-like. He has said: "basic
WordPress template", "no personality", "AI generated shit", "too plain".

**Things he has rejected repeatedly, across all projects:**
numbered prefixes (01 / 02) · decorative accent lines and hairline bars · vertical rotated
section labels · oversized quote marks · always-rounded cards · horizontal row compositions ·
coloured section backgrounds · colour washes behind cards · emoji anywhere · centred-mark +
dark-blur + cyan-glow (my own convergent default) · curtain animations reused as a pattern.

### The bar

> *"it doesnt have to be overly complicated, but i want the client to feel like yeah man,
> we've taken it to the next level for them"*

"Simple/clean/minimal" is not a goal. Every section should earn its place.

### Use the skills

> *"man we created a skill to ensure those things would never happen again. why dont you
> take it into account?"*

The skills and rules exist because a bug already cost hours. Load the matching one before
debugging, not after failing.
